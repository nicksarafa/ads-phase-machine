The teardown of ads running in this niche. Overrides the built-in block in

---

PROVEN HOOK PATTERNS — reverse-engineered from ads currently running in this
niche, ranked by how long they have run and how many variants were built.
Use the structure. Never reuse a competitor's wording.

[audience-callout] Audience call-out first
  structure: Open by naming the reader's role, then a dash, then the claim. The first three words disqualify everyone else.
  why it works: In a feed, relevance beats cleverness. Naming the role does the targeting the algorithm cannot, and the reader self-selects before the scroll completes.
  for us: 'Ops leads -', 'If you run a marketing team -', 'Agency owners -'. Light School's audiences are far more specific than 'businesses'.
[category-negation] Negate the category, not the competitor
  structure: Name the thing the reader already does and dismiss it, then offer the replacement. 'Forget X. Here is what happens instead.'
  why it works: Attacks a habit rather than a rival, so it never reads as a swipe and cannot be rebutted. It also pre-frames the offer as a category change, which sidesteps feature comparison.
  for us: The strongest available: 'Forget the AI webinar your team will not finish.' Light School's real differentiator is that people leave with a working thing.
[three-step-mechanism] Numbered three-step mechanism
  structure: Show the whole process as exactly three numbered steps. Never four. Never prose.
  why it works: Converts an abstract promise into something the reader can picture themselves completing. Three reads as easy; five reads as a project.
  for us: '1. Bring a problem your team actually has. 2. Build the thing in the session. 3. Leave with it running.'
[objection-parallel] Objection handled in parallel structure
  structure: Two or three lines of 'Need to X? Simply Y.' Same rhythm each time, one objection per line.
  why it works: Answers the doubts in the order they arrive without ever conceding they are problems. The repetition makes the product feel systematically easy rather than defensively justified.
  for us: 'Nobody on the team codes? That is the point. Everyone is busy? It is one afternoon.'
[proof-by-specificity] Proof by naming real things
  structure: Replace the adjective with the proper noun. Not 'real tools' — name the tools. Not 'saved money' — name the amount.
  why it works: Specificity is unfakeable in a way that superlatives are not. A reader cannot verify 'industry-leading' but instantly believes a named tool and a real number.
  for us: Light School has better raw material than anyone in this set: the $800/month SaaS tool a student replaced, the booking system, the Monday report.
[social-proof-ps] Social proof as a P.S.
  structure: End with 'P.S.' and one hard number. Never open with it.
  why it works: A P.S. is read at a higher rate than body copy, and proof lands better after the pitch than before it — by then the reader is looking for permission, not persuasion.
  for us: 'P.S. More than 1,300 people have been through this. Every one left with something running.'
[cost-of-inaction] Price the status quo
  structure: Put a number on what continuing to do nothing costs — in money, or in hours per week.
  why it works: Reframes the purchase as recovering an existing loss rather than adding a new expense. Loss aversion does the work.
  for us: 'Someone on your team spends six hours a week moving numbers between spreadsheets.' Team training is the offer where this is strongest — the buyer already pays the cost in salary.
[time-stamp] Stamp the year
  structure: 'It's 2026, and you are still doing X.'
  why it works: Manufactures obsolescence without insulting the reader. The implied judgment is on the method, not on them.
  for us: Use sparingly — it is the most imitated line in the niche and is on its way to being wallpaper.
[identity-over-process] Elevate to identity
  structure: Short, declarative, no mechanism. Tell the reader what they are, not what the product does.
  why it works: Skips the feature conversation entirely. Works on senior buyers who resent being sold a tool but respond to being understood.
  for us: Fits team training better than any other offer — the buyer is a leader deciding what their team becomes, not a user buying software.
[gap-live-hands-on] The gap nobody is filling
  structure: Position against self-paced learning: a room, a real problem, a thing that exists at the end.
  why it works: Not a hook pattern so much as a positioning finding, and the most valuable line in this file.
  for us: The category is uncontested in this data. Lead with what only a live session can promise — your team's actual problem, solved in the room, with the thing still running on Monday.

Across the batch, use a different pattern for each ad. Note which pattern
each ad uses in its rationale so performance can be attributed back to it.
